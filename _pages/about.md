I study learning in humans and machines, in individuals and in groups.

My research is built on questions about how people learn, teach, communicate, and work together to solve problems. I build neural network models of the cognitive and neural mechanisms that support these capacities, and I build AI tools to improve human learning and cooperation. I also study large language models from a cognitive science perspective, and investigate the impact of AI use in science.

I am currently a postdoctoral researcher in [Chris Summerfield’s group](https://humaninformationprocessing.com/) at the University of Oxford, and in December 2026 I will join the [School of Psychology and Neuroscience](https://www.gla.ac.uk/schools/psychologyneuroscience/) at the University of Glasgow as a Lecturer. I did my PhD at the University of Montreal, at the International Laboratory for Brain, Music & Sound Research (BRAMS) and Mila (the Quebec AI Institute), where I compared representations in artificial neural networks and the human auditory system.

#### Where I'm heading

Cognitive science has traditionally studied the abilities of individuals in isolation. But in real life, we rarely do anything truly alone: we are always learning and working together. Humanity's greatest achievements come from our capacity to learn from and with each other and to organize ourselves around shared goals, and many of the defining crises of our time reflect our failure to do so. 
<!-- There is also mounting evidence that what is uniquely human about our learning and reasoning is inextricably linked to our sociality. -->

In my own lab, I will continue to study the computational principles of human learning while centering the fact that all human learning is socially embedded. I want to know what separates the wisdom of crowds from the madness of mobs. As Nick Chater and George Loewenstein write in _It's On You_, paraphrasing Mancur Olson, "the concentrated interests of the few are so powerful because they can most easily be aligned and coordinated to carry through a coherent plan of action. But coordinated action by the many, once achieved, is unstoppable." In a hyperconnected, globalized world, our actions have far-reaching effects that are difficult to anticipate. We did not evolve to reason in such a world. I want to do research that supports the collective intelligence and coordinated action of the many, perhaps with the help of new technologies.
